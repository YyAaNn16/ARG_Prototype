Building a Resilient Life: An Interview with Lucas Cheng ('16)

Published by MUST Alumni Association | March 12, 2026

MUST: Lucas, it is a pleasure to connect. Your journey from a MUST Operations Management graduate to managing core operations in Bangkok is fascinating. How did your university years prepare you?

Lucas Cheng: My time at MUST gave me the structural foundation for my mind. I spent countless hours on the basketball court with my buddies, which taught me teamwork. Academically, Operations Management taught me about "systemic integrity." When you're managing complex projects, you realize that absolute perfection doesn't exist; what matters is how you rebuild order out of a broken situation.

MUST: Looking back at your career and your move across borders, what advice would you give to the current MUST juniors who are about to enter the professional world?

Lucas: Do not fear the collapse of a system. In the real business world, unpredictable variables are the norm. I've faced moments where it felt like everything was falling apart, forcing me to relocate and start completely over. So, to the juniors: embrace the chaos. Use the structural thinking you learned at MUST to "solve" crises, not avoid them. Every trial and error is just a step toward building a more resilient framework for your life.

MUST: Speaking of life frameworks, we heard some wonderful news regarding your personal life as well. Congratulations on your recent engagement!

Lucas: Thank you. Actually, she has witnessed many turning points in my life.

MUST: That sounds like a very profound connection. To be your "rock," she must understand you incredibly well.

Lucas: Exactly. In the real business world, even the most precise operational models can crash, but she always helps me sort through the chaos. It’s rare to find someone who has been on the same wavelength as you right from the starting line, growing together. To document this journey that spans many years, we recently built a private website called "Our Eternal Aurora". To me, she is the only certain cornerstone in a world of unknown variables.
